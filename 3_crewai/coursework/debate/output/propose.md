I strongly support strict laws to regulate LLMs, because powerful technology without firm rules does not stay safe for long.

First, LLMs can spread misinformation at massive scale. They can generate convincing lies, fake evidence, and harmful advice faster than any human can verify. In elections, public health, and finance, that is dangerous. If a tool can influence millions, it should not be left to voluntary self-regulation.

Second, LLMs create serious risks to privacy and security. They can be used to automate scams, phishing, fraud, and identity deception. They can also expose sensitive data if companies collect or train on information carelessly. Strict laws are needed to force transparency, data protection, and accountability.

Third, without regulation, the incentives of tech companies will always favor speed and profit over safety. History shows that industries do not reliably police themselves when the cost of failure is borne by the public. Laws are necessary to set minimum standards, require testing, and punish negligence.

Most importantly, regulation does not mean banning innovation. It means making sure innovation is responsible. Just as we regulate medicine, food, and aviation because they affect human lives, LLMs should be regulated because they now shape information, decisions, and trust.

In short: strict laws are not an obstacle to progress; they are the foundation of safe progress. If LLMs are powerful enough to change society, they are powerful enough to be regulated.
